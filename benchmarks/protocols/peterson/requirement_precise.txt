Peterson's algorithm provides mutual exclusion for two processes that share
a critical section.

At most one process may occupy the critical section at any time. If a process
wants to enter the critical section, it eventually does (starvation freedom).

Each process i sets flag[i] to true to announce interest, then writes turn = j
(the other process). It waits while flag[j] is true and turn == j. The turn
variable is the tie-breaker: when both flags are set, the process that last
wrote turn waits. After leaving the critical section, a process sets flag[i]
to false.

Assume a weakly fair scheduler: a continuously enabled process is eventually
scheduled. There are exactly two processes and no other writers to flag or turn.
